Peru is one of the world’s richest centers of cacao biodiversity, home to 16 of the 25 genetic cacao groups identified in Latin America (APP Cacao). Native to the Amazon rainforest, cacao later expanded into the Andean valleys, where altitude, fertile soils, and unique microclimates create the ideal conditions for cultivating fine aroma cacao. At Amazon Andes, we work with native cacao grown in traditional agroforestry systems that protect biodiversity, regenerate soils, and support local farming communities. The result is a cacao that carries both heritage and flavor—an authentic taste of the Amazon, nurtured by the Andes.

The Distinction of Peruvian Criollo Cacao

Less than 5% of the world's cacao is Criollo. Understand why this ancestral variety is chosen for ceremonial purposes and high-end products.

Our Peruvian Criollo cacao, "The Food of the Gods" for our ancestors, is notably distinguished from conventional African cacao. Its sensory profile and artisanal process make it the preferred choice for ceremonial experiences and premium chocolates.

Characteristic Peruvian Criollo Cacao (Amazon Andes) Common African Cacao
AROMA Soft, floral bouquet with delicate tropical-fruit notes. More bitter and astringent character.
FLAVOR Smooth and delicate, with hints of tropical fruits, flowers, and nuts. Pronounced acidity and stronger bitterness.
PROCESS Artisanal production preserving natural cacao flavors through traditional fermentation and sun-drying. Industrial-scale fermentation, mechanical drying, and mass-processing methods.
USAGE Valued for gourmet quality—ideal for high-end chocolates, premium confections, and ceremonial beverages. Commonly used in bulk cocoa powder, cocoa butter, and mass-market chocolate products.

CACAO CULTIVATION

Our premium Criollo cacao is cultivated in the fertile valleys of the Peruvian Andes. Farmers carefully tend to cacao trees that are grown at optimal altitudes between 800-1,200 meters.

SUSTAINABLE FARMING

Our cacao comes from sustainable farms managed by Amazonian communities in Peru. We practice regenerative agriculture that preserves biodiversity and ensures long-term soil health.

BEAN HARVESTING

Cacao pods are carefully hand-harvested when fully ripe. The timing is crucial as it affects the flavor profile. Each pod contains 20-40 beans surrounded by sweet pulp that contributes to the fermentation process.

FERMENTATION & DRYING

Beans undergo traditional fermentation for 5-7 days, developing complex flavors. They are then sun-dried slowly to preserve ceremonial properties and achieve optimal moisture content.

PROCESSING & PACKAGING

Dried beans are carefully processed into ceremonial-grade cacao paste, powder, or nibs. Products are packaged in food-grade containers that preserve freshness and sacred properties.

Sustainable Work

Sustainable management
of Ceremonial Cacao

AMAZON ANDES and PROFONANPE (the largest private environmental fund in Perú) have signed a cooperation agreement to promote sustainable cacao cultivation and forest conservation in the Peruvian Andes. We work directly with indigenous farming communities to preserve traditional organic farming methods while protecting the unique biodiversity of the region. Our partner communities maintain ancestral knowledge of Criollo cacao cultivation, ensuring the highest quality ceremonial grade cacao while implementing sustainable agroforestry practices. In 2023, we expanded our organic cacao cultivation area by 30%, supporting over 200 farming families and preserving over 1,000 hectares of native forest through our sustainable agriculture program.
